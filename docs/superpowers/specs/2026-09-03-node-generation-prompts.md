# Node (Eave-Chime) — image generation prompt pack

Companion to `2026-09-03-node-mascot-concept.md` (Direction 1, chosen 2026-09-03).
For an open-weights image model — FLUX is the assumed target; the prompts are written in plain
descriptive language, which is what FLUX, Ideogram and Gemini-family models want. Midjourney
variants are in §7.

---

## 0. The two rules that matter most

**1. Never write the word "Pokémon", "Chimecho", "wind chime Pokémon", or any species name in a
prompt.** Those tokens pull the model straight back onto existing designs — which is the exact
failure being corrected. Describe the *style traits* instead (§2). The concept is original; the
prompt has to be too.

**2. Generate the creature on a plain background first, not in the scene.** A clean cut-out is
what makes the hover animation possible, and compositing a cut-out into the scene is far easier
than trying to extract a creature from a busy generated workshop. Scene generation is §5 and is
a separate job.

---

## 1. The character — base description

Reuse this block verbatim in every prompt so the creature stays consistent across poses.

> A small hand-thrown ceramic creature shaped like a rounded, slightly lopsided gourd,
> hanging from a single braided fabric cord tied in a simple overhand knot above it. Warm
> terracotta clay body with a cream underside. It has a small friendly face in the upper third:
> two small dark ochre eyes and one soft curved seam for a mouth. A narrow horizontal slot low
> on its body reveals a small brass-brown teardrop weight hanging inside on its own short
> thread. Two small soft indigo fabric pennant flags hang from its sides. One small carved
> wooden hexagonal bead is threaded onto the cord knot above it.

**Proportion note to include if the model drifts:** *the body is about two thirds as tall as it
is wide, and the face sits high on it — this is a small cupped-in-two-hands object, not a large
bell.*

---

## 2. Style block

> Trading card illustration style. Bold confident dark brown outlines of varying weight, heavier
> on the silhouette than on interior detail. Flat cel shading with hard-edged shadow shapes, not
> soft gradients. Warm saturated palette. Soft halftone dot texture. Friendly, cosy, storybook
> quality. Single warm directional light from the upper left. Clean vector-like finish.

Outline colour should be a warm brown-black, never pure black or neutral grey — that single
choice is a large part of why the reference cards read warm.

---

## 3. Pose prompts

Generate the **rest** pose first. Once you have one you like, produce every other pose by
*editing that image* (Flux Kontext, or your model's edit mode) rather than by re-prompting from
scratch — that is the only reliable way to get poses that are the same creature.

### 3a. Rest — the hero image

> [CHARACTER BLOCK] The creature hangs perfectly still and calm, its cord vertical. Both eyes
> are closed in soft contented downward crescents. The brass weight inside hangs straight down,
> motionless. The two side pennants hang limp and relaxed. It is peaceful and asleep.
> [STYLE BLOCK] Centred, full body, plain flat cream background, soft contact shadow beneath.

### 3b. Awake — generated by *editing* 3a

> Keep everything identical. Open its eyes into small round friendly eyes with a tiny warm
> catchlight in each. Tilt the whole body very slightly to one side as if it has just swung.
> Lift the two side pennants outward as if a breeze just arrived.

### 3c. Ear/pennant turn — the devotion beat

> Keep everything identical. Turn only the near side pennant so it angles toward the viewer.

### 3d. Three-quarter and profile views — for the LoRA only

> Keep the creature identical. Show it from a three-quarter angle. / Show it in profile.

## 4. Layers — what to cut, and why

The hover animation needs these as **separate transparent PNGs**, all registered to the same
canvas so they stack without re-alignment:

| Layer | Moves on hover | Pivot |
|---|---|---|
| Cord + knot + hex bead | No — fixed anchor | — |
| Body shell (eyes removed) | Swings as a pendulum | Top of body, at the cord |
| Eyes closed | Cross-fades out | — |
| Eyes open | Cross-fades in | — |
| Inner weight / clapper | Swings on a faster period | Its own thread top |
| Left pennant | Lifts and flutters | Body attachment point |
| Right pennant | Lifts and flutters, phase-offset | Body attachment point |

If the model can output layers natively, take them. Otherwise generate the rest pose, then use
the edit mode to produce "the same image with the pennants removed", "…with only the pennants on
a plain background", and so on — editing out is usually cleaner than masking by hand.

**A hanging object was chosen partly because it barely self-occludes** — cord above, body in the
middle, weight below, pennants to the sides. Keep it that way: do not accept a generation where
a pennant crosses in front of the body, because that seam is unmaskable.

## 5. Scene — separate job, generate after the creature is locked

The card frame is **242 × 346**. Two hard composition constraints:

- **The lower third must be pale and low-contrast.** Card text sits over it in dark ink with no
  opaque panel, exactly as real full-art cards do. Prompt for *"the lower portion of the image is
  bright, pale and low in contrast — pale wood, cream paper, soft light."*
- **The upper strip is covered** by the card name and HP, so nothing important goes in the top
  ~20%.

Scene prompt seed (restaged for a hanging creature — the previous mid-repair staging assumed an
object resting on a desk and no longer applies):

> A warm cluttered maker's workbench in late afternoon. Low golden sun rakes through a window on
> the left. A pegboard wall crowded with hand tools, jars of screws, coils of wire, a small
> trailing plant, a stack of books. In the foreground a wooden desk, pale and sunlit, with a
> notebook, a pale ceramic mug and a small parts tray. Warm, lived-in, quiet.
> [STYLE BLOCK]

The creature is composited in afterwards, hanging from a small hook at the edge of the desk or
the corner of the window frame — at the literal periphery, at hand height.

---

## 6. Negative prompt

Carry this on every generation. The first line is the one that matters.

> anime mascot, existing cartoon character, bell shape, trumpet flare, purple banding, suction
> cup, ribbon tail, stubby limbs, 3d render, photorealistic, neon, glowing circuitry, chrome,
> dark background, gradient background, lens flare, text, watermark, signature, busy background,
> multiple characters, cluttered silhouette

Bell shape / trumpet flare / purple banding / suction cup / ribbon tail are there specifically
because they are the identifying markers of the nearest real creature. If they appear, the
originality audit in the concept brief has been violated and the generation should be rejected.

## 7. Midjourney variants

Same content, Midjourney's dialect. Put the style block first, subject second.

```
trading card creature illustration, bold warm dark outlines, flat cel shading, warm terracotta
palette, halftone texture, cosy storybook :: small hand-thrown ceramic gourd-shaped creature
hanging from a braided cord, closed contented crescent eyes, small brass weight visible through
a slot in its body, two small indigo fabric pennants at its sides, plain cream background
--ar 1:1 --style raw --no bell, flare, purple bands, text, 3d
```

Then for every subsequent pose, pass the chosen rest image as `--cref <url>` with `--cw 100` to
hold the character.

## 8. Consistency bootstrap

There is a chicken-and-egg here: a LoRA needs images of Node, and Node does not exist yet.

1. Generate a batch from §3a. Pick **one**.
2. Use edit mode (§3b–3d) to make ~20 variants of that one image — poses, angles, the awake
   state, the pennant turn.
3. Train a LoRA on those 20 (ai-toolkit or kohya_ss, roughly 1500–2500 steps).
4. Every card and pose from then on calls the LoRA and stays on-model.

Step 2 doubles as the animation frame set, so it is not wasted work either way.

---

## 9. Acceptance checklist

Reject a generation that fails any of these:

- [ ] A person who knows the TCG would say "some kind of chime creature", not name a species.
- [ ] No bell flare, no suction-cup nub, no ribbon tail, no purple banding.
- [ ] Outlines are warm brown-black, not pure black or grey, and vary in weight.
- [ ] Shadows are hard-edged shapes, not soft gradients.
- [ ] No pennant crosses in front of the body (unmaskable seam).
- [ ] It reads as a creature — a face you feel something about — at 50px, not just at full size.
- [ ] No gold on the creature itself. Gilding belongs to the frame and background.

The last two are the ones that killed the previous attempts. Check them at actual size, on the
card, next to `01-magneton-lineage/Magnemite_Ultra_Rare_gorgeous_perfect_example_of_story_on_card.jpg`
— not in isolation at full resolution.
