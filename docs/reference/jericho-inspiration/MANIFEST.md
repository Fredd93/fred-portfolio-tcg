# Fred's reference set — what's here and what he flagged

18 images, supplied by Fred 2026-09-02, sorted into the craft buckets described in
`README.md`. **Three carry his own flags in the filename.** Those flags are the taste
signal and outrank anything inferred from research.

## The three flagged cards

### 1. `01-magneton-lineage/Magnemite_Ultra_Rare_gorgeous_perfect_example_of_story_on_card.jpg`
**Magnemite 242/236, Cosmic Eclipse, illus. Fumie Kitaoka (2019).** Fred's flag: *"gorgeous,
perfect example of story on card."*

A mechanic/engineer grins at the viewer, wiping his brow, mid-repair on a Magnemite that
floats at his hand. More Magnemite drift behind him; a workshop of shelved parts fills the
background. Warm oranges, browns, olive drab. Text sits directly on the illustration — no
opaque panel.

**What it establishes:** the human is not a background element here — he is the *largest and
most expressive thing in the frame*, and the card's story is a shared moment of ordinary
work between a person and their machine. This validates the v2 brief's core finding (the
flavor text needs two characters) but pushes considerably further than the brief's staging,
which had Fred small, faceless, and cropped at the frame edge.

### 2. `01-magneton-lineage/Magnezone_Promo_my_favorite_asthetically.jpg`
**Magnezone GG18/GG70, Crown Zenith Galarian Gallery, illus. Shinya Komatsu (2023).**
Fred's flag: *"my favorite aesthetically."*

Flat graphic illustration — bold outlines, flat color fills, almost mid-century-poster or
children's-book. A dense machine world of pipes, conveyor belts, gears and floating
Magnemite fills every part of the frame. Tight cool palette: teal, mint, soft purple, cream.
Full bleed, text directly on art.

**What it establishes:** a whole *world* — you could look at this card for a minute and keep
finding things. It is playful and inviting rather than dramatic, and the machine world feels
like somewhere pleasant to be. Note that the density here is achieved by packing the frame
with props and repeated motifs rather than by rendering technique — a useful observation
about *how* the charm is delivered, but see the section below: do not mistake the mechanism
for the reason Fred chose it.

### 3. `01-magneton-lineage/Magnezone_SIR_fan_art_Best_candidate.png`
**Magnezone ex, Special Illustration Rare — fan art.** Fred's flag: *"best candidate."*

Rainbow hyper-rare frame. A pastel radial ray-burst behind a centered, dominant Magnezone.
The entire background is a packed field of repeated motifs — scattered horseshoe magnets,
screws, Magnemite faces — over a flat teal city silhouette. Bold black outlines, flat fills,
three gold rarity stars.

**What it establishes:** the closest single reference to Jericho's target — top rarity tier,
correct lineage, radial burst present, and density achieved through **repeated motif fields**
rather than rendering technique. Note it is fan art, so it is a taste reference, not evidence
of official convention.

## What Fred is actually selecting for — read this before drawing conclusions

An earlier version of this manifest claimed Fred's picks pointed to a *rendering technique*
(flat graphic vs. painterly). **He corrected that directly, and the correction matters:**

> *"I don't have a favorite based on flatness or not. My favorite is based on the aesthetic
> and emotions it evokes — for example the Magnemite looks warm and cute and fuzzy all
> together, literally makes me feel things when I look at it."*

So the selection criterion is **emotional, not technical.** Do not read these three cards as
a style guide and reverse-engineer a technique spec from them. That inversion — taking a felt
response, reducing it to mechanics, then optimising for the mechanics — is the exact process
that produces work which satisfies a checklist and moves nobody. It is what produced v1.

**The target for Jericho is a feeling.** Technique is downstream and serves it; any technique
that delivers the feeling is legitimate, and any technique that doesn't is wasted effort no
matter how rigorous.

What generates the warmth in the Magnemite specifically: a person **grinning**, sweaty,
caught mid-repair; his machine floating close at his hand rather than across the room; a
cluttered, lived-in workshop; warm saturated colour. You can tell the man *likes* this
Magnemite. The affection between them is the subject of the illustration.

That is what Jericho has to earn. Node isn't a sentinel standing guard — the flavor text
("lives at the periphery until you need it — then it's already listening") describes something
quietly devoted. Warmth and companionship, not surveillance.

## Secondary observation (technique-level, subordinate to the above)

All three picks are **bright and saturated.** None is dark. The v2 brief's "Late shift" scene
is a dim room lit by a single lamp — which risks reproducing the failure the brief itself
diagnosed in §0.4 ("a dark-mode UI panel with furniture drawn on it"), only in more detail.
Worth defending explicitly or dropping.
